# K-lawus Project Brief

## 1. Mission

K-lawus is a Korean-American community safety platform built to prevent financial fraud, document patterns of abuse, and help victims act quickly with evidence, not rumor.

The platform focuses on:

- financial fraud and malicious non-repayment
- contractor and service scams
- landlord-tenant abuse and housing disputes
- repeat misconduct that can be supported by records, documents, or multiple corroborated reports

## 2. Core Positioning

K-lawus should **not** be a reckless blacklist.

It should be positioned as:

- an evidence-centered prevention platform
- a victim support and reporting hub
- a public-record and scam-pattern library
- a trusted referral channel to lawyers and vetted service providers

## 3. Editorial Principles

1. Behavior, not ethnicity
   The platform must never target a race, ethnicity, religion, or nationality. It documents conduct and evidence only.

2. Evidence before amplification
   Reports should be categorized by verification level before publication.

3. Victim safety first
   The platform should help users preserve documents, timelines, and reporting options immediately.

4. Clear due-process mechanisms
   There must be correction, appeal, and response procedures.

5. Privacy discipline
   Sensitive personal data such as SSN, bank account numbers, home addresses, children's data, and immigration details must never be published.

## 4. Recommended Verification Levels

- `Received`: report submitted, not publicly visible or only internally stored
- `Screened`: basic moderation complete, waiting for more proof
- `Corroborated`: multiple matching submissions or substantial documentary support
- `Public Record Confirmed`: linked to court filings, judgments, liens, or official records
- `Resolved`: settlement, retraction, repayment, or court disposition recorded

## 5. Scope of Coverage

### A. Fraud and financial abuse

- unpaid personal loans with evidence of bad-faith intent
- fake investments
- contractor deposit theft
- business partnership fraud
- vehicle and marketplace scams

### B. Housing and landlord-tenant issues

- security deposit abuse
- unlawful eviction pressure
- undisclosed habitability issues
- repeated lease fraud
- tenant damage or nonpayment patterns supported by court records
- fake rental listings

### C. Community protection education

- scam playbooks
- warning signs
- evidence collection guides
- step-by-step reporting instructions

## 6. MVP Product Areas

### Public-facing

- homepage with mission, warning categories, and trust framework
- scam pattern library
- housing dispute resource section
- “how to report” guide
- lawyer and service partner directory

### Intake and moderation

- structured victim report form
- evidence upload workflow
- moderation queue
- publication checklist and verification labels

### Trust and legal pages

- editorial standards
- takedown / correction request process
- response and appeal policy
- privacy and terms

## 7. Revenue Model

Primary revenue should come from:

- attorney advertising
- housing and consumer-rights service listings
- document review or evidence-packaging services
- community sponsorships

Avoid:

- pay-to-remove allegations
- anonymous paid smear submissions
- sensational ad inventory that weakens trust

## 8. Risks and Safeguards

### Legal risks

- defamation
- privacy violations
- harassment / retaliation claims
- inaccurate identity matching

### Safeguards

- human moderation before publication
- strict claim taxonomy
- evidence threshold for naming individuals
- legal review before broad public rollout
- immutable audit trail for edits and removals

## 9. Launch Strategy

Phase 1:

- launch as a prevention and reporting hub
- publish scam patterns and housing guides
- collect reports privately

Phase 2:

- add structured public cases with verification labels
- publish public-record backed profiles only

Phase 3:

- build a stronger referral marketplace
- publish community alerts and multilingual guides

## 10. Immediate Build Goal

The first shipped version should demonstrate:

- clear mission
- strong trust posture
- well-defined coverage areas
- safe reporting flow
- monetization direction without undermining credibility
